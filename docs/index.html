India · AI policy tracker

What are Indian states doing on AI?

Every state says it wants to lead on AI. We went through thousands of news stories and the policy documents themselves to see who is actually doing something.

The rule we used: a state government has to do something. Set a policy, spend money, open an institution, put AI to work, sign a deal or write a rule. Speeches don't count.

One caution: more headlines doesn't mean more work. Some states simply get more press.

    What counts

    We count something when a state government body acts on AI. That means a department, a minister, the Chief Minister or cabinet, the state police, a state agency or PSU, a state-run hospital or university, or a district administration. "Acts" means one of six things: a policy, money, an institution, a deployment, an agreement or a rule.

    In

    • A cabinet approving an AI policy
    • State police rolling out AI cameras
    • An MoU the state signs with a company
    • Money set aside for an AI mission
    • A state university starting an AI course

    Out

    • Company news that happens to be in a state
    • Central schemes with no state partner
    • Speeches and opinion pieces
    • A single deepfake crime case

    Three grey areas, and the call we made on each:

    CourtsLeft out of the policy layer. A High Court's AI guidelines matter, but courts aren't the state government.
    AI summits a state hostsKept when the state is the one acting. Most end up under "Everything else", since a summit on its own isn't a policy.
    City corporationsKept only when a state government body is named in the story.

    Where the data comes from

    News. Google News RSS. We run six searches per state ("Karnataka AI policy", "Karnataka AI MoU" and so on) once for every quarter since January 2025, so each quarter gets its own limit of about 100 results. That came to results from searches. Google News is free, needs no key and reaches back to 2025. The free plans of NewsAPI and GNews only search the last 30 days, which rules them out.

    Policy documents. A research snapshot of AI policies, missions, guidelines and laws across India (the README says where it came from). We kept the state and UT documents that are actually about AI, and set aside central ones, court orders, that weren't really about AI (data-centre and gig-work rules, mostly) and placeholder.

    From a story to an event

    1. Collect search results.
    2. Remove copies. The same article often turns up in several searches. are left.
    3. Keep the relevant ones. The headline needs an AI word and a sign of a state government: a minister, a department, or a state named as the one acting ("Karnataka signs…"). pass.
    4. Pin down the state from the headline, not from the search that found it, and drop stories about other states or from before 2025. remain.
    5. Merge duplicates into events. events.
    6. Label each event with a category, a stage and a confidence level.

    Telling states apart

    UP, U.P., Uttar-Pradesh, Uttarpradesh and उत्तर प्रदेश are one state here. Names are compared letter by letter, ignoring spaces and hyphens. Short forms like UP and TN only match in capitals, so "sets up" never turns into Uttar Pradesh. A state's name outweighs a city in it: in "Bengaluru firm signs AI MoU with Telangana govt", the state is Telangana. When no state is named at all, we use the state we searched for and flag the row ( events).

    Spotting duplicates

    Ten outlets covering one announcement should count once. Two stories merge when they're about the same state, came out within three days of each other, and share most of their distinctive words. Two rules stop bad merges. Different companies never merge: a Microsoft deal and a Google deal are two events. And a reversal never merges with the original. Uttar Pradesh signing a ₹25,000 crore MoU with Puch AI and then cancelling it are two events here, not one.

    That made merges, each listed in data/dedupe_report.txt. It still misses some. Odisha's AI policy approval shows up twice, because the Times of India and New Indian Express headlines share too few words.

    Categories and stages

    Category comes from keywords, and the first match wins, in the order of the buckets above. Stage comes from the first telling verb in the headline: "to set up" is proposed, "signs" or "approves" is announced, "launches" or "deploys" is implemented. When there's no such verb we say "Unclear" rather than guess.

    How sure are we?

    ●●● HighAn official source, or three or more outlets.
    ●●○ MediumA major outlet, or two outlets.
    ●○○ LowOne outlet.

    This measures how well a story is backed up, not whether it's true. Three papers running the same press release still count as three. Low-confidence rows stay on the page on purpose. Treat them as leads, not facts.

    Dates

    A news date is the day the first story in the group was published, in Indian time. That's usually within a day or two of the event, but not always. Policy documents use the date they were notified or approved, and when only the month or year is known, that's all we show.

    What we can't see

    • Only English headlines, plus one Hindi search for Uttar Pradesh. Plenty of state news is in Kannada, Telugu, Tamil or Odia, and we miss it. Odisha probably loses the most.
    • We read headlines, not articles. Paywalls and Google's redirect links make full text hard to get, so each label rests on one line of text.
    • Google returns about 100 results per search, and we don't know how it ranks them.
    • Hardly any official sources made it into the news layer, so "high confidence" mostly means three outlets ran it.
    • Stage is a guess from verbs. "Unclear" is common, and we'd rather say so.
    • Chief Ministers change. Two of our five states got a new one in 2026.
    • Run the searches tomorrow and you'll get a slightly different set. data/raw.jsonl is the snapshot behind everything here.

    Keeping it fresh

    A daily job reruns the searches and opens a pull request, so a person reads the new rows before they go live. Mistakes a rule can't fix go into data/overrides.json, each with a written reason. We also hand-check a random sample; the numbers are in audit/results.md.
